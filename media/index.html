The gap in the stack

Why current tools stop too early

Each layer of the modern stack does its own job well — and stops before the decision is actually governed, authorized, executed, and learned from. None of this is a criticism of any particular vendor; it is a description of where each category ends.

Dashboards

Report that performance moved, in near-real time and in detail. They describe the movement — they do not diagnose its cause or govern the response.

STOPS AT: REPORTING

Attribution models

Estimate how credit should be divided across touchpoints. Credit division is not a complete causal operating model — it does not test counterfactual actions or carry constraints.

STOPS AT: CREDIT ESTIMATION

Customer data platforms

Organize customer data into profiles and audiences. Organizing data is not the same as governing cross-functional commercial decisions about media, margin, and inventory.

STOPS AT: DATA ORGANIZATION

In-platform bid automation

Optimizes efficiently inside one platform's auction — but may not see your margin, inventory position, site experience, finance constraints, or policy boundaries.

STOPS AT: THE PLATFORM WALL

Data warehouses

Retain the data, durably and completely. What they do not retain is the complete chain of reasoning, authorization, action, and outcome — the record of why a decision was made, who approved it, what was done, and what happened next. That chain is what MIZ OKI Media preserves.

STOPS AT: STORAGE

The substrate

The Growth Decision Graph

Every decision travels one governed pathway through the graph. Each stage is a first-class object — inspectable, explainable, and preserved.

Signal

A change worth attention — media metrics, commerce events, experience telemetry, inventory movement, or an economic threshold.

Entity

The signal resolves to the real things it concerns: campaigns, products, audiences, pages, warehouses, ledgers.

Cause

Candidate explanations are tested against the evidence — with the supporting and disconfirming evidence preserved.

Objective

What the business is actually optimizing here — incremental profit, margin protection, growth within constraints.

Policy

Financial floors, brand rules, risk boundaries, and authority thresholds that any proposed action must satisfy.

Action

The authorized intervention — scoped, bounded, dispatched to the executing system, and recorded.

Outcome

The realized result, compared against the prediction, written back into memory.

A conventional knowledge graph explains what is connected. The MIZ OKI Decision Graph preserves the evidence, causal reasoning, objective, policy, decision, action, and realized outcome — so the organization can govern and learn from decisions.

How it works

The complete causal decision trace

Every MIZ OKI Media decision moves through the platform's seven-stage operating loop, in this exact order — and every stage leaves a record.

SENSE → REASON → PLAN → VALIDATE → DECIDE → ACT → LEARN

  1. 1 · SENSEIdentifies what changed — ingesting signals across media, commerce, experience, inventory, and economics into structured evidence.
  2. 2 · REASONEvaluates likely causes — testing candidate explanations against the evidence in the Decision Graph rather than assuming the loudest metric is the culprit.
  3. 3 · PLANProduces counterfactual choices — the plausible actions, compared against each other and against doing nothing.
  4. 4 · VALIDATEApplies financial, operational, ethical, policy, and risk constraints. Every proposed action can be evaluated against configured policy and authority boundaries before dispatch.
  5. 5 · DECIDERoutes the recommendation to the correct authority — autonomous only where explicitly granted, and to a named human everywhere else.
  6. 6 · ACTDispatches only an authorized action — scoped, bounded, and with its rollback path preserved.
  7. 7 · LEARNCompares predicted and realized outcomes, writing the result into outcome memory so calibration compounds.

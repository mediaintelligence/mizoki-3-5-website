## 3. The SRPVDAL Canon

SRPVDAL is the operating spine of MIZOKI3. It is the most important architecture, product, and creative truth.

The only approved sequence is:

**Sense -> Reason -> Plan -> Validate -> Decide -> Act -> Learn**

Do not use older five-stage language. Do not remove Plan. Do not replace Validate with vague "verification" unless it is described as part of the Validate stage. Do not skip Learn.

| Stage | What it does | Public creative wording | Must never imply |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sense | Ingests signals from systems, contracts, ledgers, campaigns, transactions, people, and events into the graph. | "The system feels what changed." | That data is manually uploaded only. |
| Reason | Connects the signal to causal relationships, obligations, historical patterns, constraints, and predicted consequences. | "It understands why the signal matters." | That it merely summarizes data. |
| Plan | Generates candidate actions, options, rollback paths, and sequencing. | "It builds a path before it moves." | That the first idea becomes the action. |
| Validate | Runs simulations, policy checks, legal/fiduciary constraints, statistical confidence, security requirements, and risk guardrails. | "It proves the action is allowed and safe enough." | That validation is optional or cosmetic. |
| Decide | Applies the Decision Control Plane and Decision Eligibility Layer to approve, gate, reroute, or veto. | "It decides through policy, not impulse." | That an agent chooses freely without controls. |
| Act | Executes only approved, scoped, reversible, logged actions through authorized connectors. | "It acts only when authorized." | That the system can do anything it wants. |
| Learn | Compares predicted versus actual outcomes, updates memory, promotes reusable skills, and improves future decisions. | "Every outcome strengthens the next decision." | That learning is a vague marketing claim. |

## SRPVDAL narrative sentence

When MIZOKI3 senses a meaningful change, it reasons over the shared graph, plans possible moves, validates them against constraints, decides through policy, acts only when authorized, and learns from the outcome.

## SRPVDAL creative rule

Every major page, demo, pitch, and product explanation must show SRPVDAL either explicitly or implicitly. MIZOKI3 is not a collection of features; it is a governed decision loop.

## 4. Master Narrative Arc

The public-facing story should follow the same emotional and logical sequence every time.

## Act I - The old world is too slow

The enterprise has many systems, but no nervous system. Dashboards refresh after the moment has passed. CRMs store rows. Spreadsheets fragment the truth. Reports explain yesterday. People still make high-stakes decisions by reconciling stale fragments.

## Act II - The business needs living understanding

A modern enterprise needs a living graph that understands entities, relationships, obligations, money movement, risk, campaigns, customers, contracts, and time. It needs to know not only what happened, but what changed, what caused it, what will happen next, and what action is allowed.

## Act III - MIZOKI3 becomes the governed reflex arc

MIZOKI3 gives the business a nervous system. Every signal enters a shared temporal-causal graph. Every possible action moves through SRPVDAL. Every execution is simulated, validated, authorized, and logged. The result is real-time understanding, prediction, and defensible action.

## Act IV - The system adapts to the company

MIZOKI3 does not force a company into a fixed software mold. It maps the customer's actual business structure. If the company has 3 domains, it maps 3. If it has 19, it maps 19. If the business evolves, the graph and controllers evolve with it.

## Act V - The outcome compounds

The value is not a one-time insight. It is compounding decision intelligence. Each proposal, veto, approval, action, and outcome becomes part of institutional memory. The business gets faster, safer, more explainable, and more adaptive over time.

## 6. Domain Architecture Truth

The domain story must be handled carefully. MIZOKI3 should never look like a fixed suite of five departments. The example domains are examples of how a deployment may be structured.

## Canonical rule

**MIZOKI3 maps to the customer's actual business. The company does not have to map itself to MIZOKI3.**

If the customer has Legal, Treasury, Risk, Marketing, Operations, HR, Claims, Procurement, Tax, Logistics, Security, and Customer Success, the system can model those domains. If the customer has a nonstandard structure, the system can model that too.

## Example domains currently shown

| Example domain | Public label | Function in story |
|---|---|---|
| Counsel | Legal & Counsel | Contracts, covenants, obligations, litigation pathways, compliance constraints. |
| Estate | Estate & Trust | Trust structures, beneficiary impact, tax strategy, fiduciary modeling. |
| Capital | Treasury & Capital | Capital allocation, liquidity stress, covenants, reserve movements. |
| Signal | Growth & Signal | Media, attribution, acquisition, budget movement, ROI validation. |
| Risk | Risk & Compliance | Exposure, regulatory monitoring, policy constraints, continuous verification. |

## Additional domains that can be introduced

MIZOKI3 can support any decision domain that has signals, entities, constraints, actions, and outcomes. Examples: Operations, Procurement, Sales, Customer Success, HR, Security, Finance, Tax, Claims, Supply Chain, Product, Real Estate, Insurance, Healthcare Administration, Manufacturing, and Programmatic Advertising.

## Domain copy rule

Every domain must connect back to the same shared graph. Avoid describing each domain as an isolated app. The power is not that there are many apps; the power is that one intelligence layer reasons across all of them.

## Correct phrasing

- "Example domains"
- "A typical deployment may include"
- "The system maps to your actual operating structure"
- "One shared graph, many domain controllers"
- "Your deployment is whatever your business is"

## Incorrect phrasing

- "The five MIZOKI3 modules"
- "Choose one of our departments"
- "MIZOKI3 is a legal, estate, capital, signal, and risk product"
- "Each department has its own separate intelligence"

## Demo narrative standard

A good MIZOKI3 demo should tell this story:

1. A signal appears.
2. The graph connects the signal to context.
3. The system reasons over downstream consequences.
4. A plan is generated.
5. A simulation reveals risk or proves eligibility.
6. The Decision Control Plane gates, vetoes, routes, or authorizes.
7. The action is executed only if approved.
8. The outcome is logged and learned.

## ACT-991 narrative

ACT-991 is a strong demonstration because it makes governance tangible. A $5.0M capital distribution appears reasonable until the system validates it against a liquidity or fiduciary floor. The action clears some gates but fails a binding constraint. The Decision Control Plane vetoes it, reroutes the action, and writes the trace to the ledger. The lesson is clear: MIZOKI3 is not reckless automation. It is governed intelligence.

## 10. Governance, Trust, and Assurance

The more autonomous the story becomes, the more explicit the controls must become. Trust is not a supporting paragraph. It is part of the core product.

## Assurance pillars

| Pillar | Meaning | Public proof point |
|---|---|---|
| Customer-managed encryption | The customer controls encryption keys. | "Your keys, your cloud." |
| Isolated reasoning | Reasoning occurs inside controlled environments. | "Reasoning never leaves your perimeter." |
| Deterministic overrides | Human and policy controls can override or gate decisions. | "Human-in-the-loop keys gate authorized action." |
| Immutable audit trail | Every proposal, simulation, decision, and action is recorded. | "Tamper-evident ledger." |
| Policy authorization | Actions require policy eligibility, not only model confidence. | "The Decision Control Plane applies deterministic policy." |
| Reversibility and scope | Actions should be scoped, reversible where possible, and logged. | "Scoped, reversible, and written to the ledger." |

## Claim safety rules

- Any numeric metrics shown publicly must be either sourced or clearly labeled illustrative.
- Any autonomy claim must include validation, authorization, and auditability.
- Any security claim must be grounded in actual implementation or stated as target architecture.
- Avoid saying "guaranteed" unless legally approved.
- Avoid implying legal, financial, or fiduciary advice is executed without human authorization.
- Use "eligible for autonomous execution" instead of "automatically executes everything."

## Trust language hierarchy

1. **Verifiable** - the decision can be inspected.
2. **Governed** - the decision follows policy.
3. **Validated** - the action is simulated against constraints.
4. **Authorized** - execution rights are controlled.
5. **Auditable** - the trace is preserved.
6. **Learning** - outcomes improve future decisions.
